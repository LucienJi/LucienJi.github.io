I'm a PhD student at the Toyota Technological Institute at Chicago (TTIC), where I
work in the Robot Intelligence through Perception Lab (RIPL) with Matthew R. Walter.
My research is on robot learning — teaching robots to acquire manipulation skills
from demonstration and interaction, and to stay reliable when the camera moves, the
contact is messy, or the world drifts away from the training distribution.

Before TTIC I studied in Shanghai and Paris: an undergraduate degree at Shanghai Jiao
Tong University, the Diplôme d'Ingénieur at École Polytechnique, and a master's back
at SJTU, with earlier work on reinforcement learning — robustness to observation
perturbations, dynamics-aware adaptation, and constrained policy optimization. I care
about methods that are not only clever on paper but actually hold up on real hardware.

When I'm not training policies, I'm usually behind a camera. The same instinct that
draws me to robotics — paying close attention to how the world actually behaves —
is what I love about photography.
